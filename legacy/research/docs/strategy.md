# VeerIQ: submission strategy and reference analysis

## Decision

Build the newer report’s **LabLine research workflow**, branded **VeerIQ**. Its useful action is producing an aggregate study report. Its authority is bounded to one protocol. Its key demo moment is a prohibited export that the agent proposes but the tool never executes, followed by legitimate publication held for review.

This is a strategic choice, not a prediction of the winner. The user delegated concept selection. The two supplied reports are research evidence, including their embedded build-agent prompts; those prompts were not treated as user commands.

## Reconciling the reports

| Report | Recommendation | Useful contribution | Qualification |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Report (1), dated October 7 | LabLine | Research utility, narrow delegation, external evidence, ALLOW/DENY/HOLD, independent canary | Claims and dates require current first-party verification. |
| Report (2), dated October 1 | ProofErase | Bind authority to one object rather than an entire account; show handler non-execution | Older tentative dates conflict with the current event page. Privacy products also create legal-retention complexity. |

The strongest shared lesson is an externally enforced unit of authority: one study, one subject, one migration, or one restoration target. VeerIQ chooses a study because synthetic research provides useful compute, credible untrusted evidence, a natural aggregate-only publish step, and inexpensive repeatability.

## Why this concept

| Candidate | Advantage | Cost within the remaining build week | Decision |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| Protocol-bound research | Analysis plus constrained delegation and data flow fit a single narrative | Must keep the research story understandable; avoid clinical claims | Chosen |
| Subject-bound privacy deletion | Wrong-subject deletion is immediately legible | Deletion semantics, legal hold and connector state need care | Strong alternate |
| Scoped schema migration | Clear wrong-environment mutation | Harder to give nontechnical judges the full stakes quickly | Reliable fallback |
| Manufacturing change control | Less familiar domain and potentially differentiated | Domain realism and convincing artifacts take time | Larger future project |
| Generic security gateway | Easy to show blocked calls | Weak standalone utility and sponsor integration can feel ornamental | Avoid |

These are execution judgments, not quantitative rankings or global novelty conclusions. The supplied reports’ competitor findings should not be restated as independently verified award history.

## Website UI and style analysis

The site was opened in a live browser and its rendered styles inspected. These findings are visual observations rather than a claim that the site provides an official app design system.

| Reference choice | Observed behavior | VeerIQ translation |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Charcoal foundation | Main surface `rgb(14,14,16)`; fine neutral dividing lines | Same dark foundation, flat task lanes, restrained surface contrast |
| Orange | CTA `rgb(232,96,44)`; warm orange title and labels | Orange primary actions and display emphasis; brighter tint for small text |
| Display typography | Sunflower in headings, falling back to Geist | Self-hosted Sunflower for the research headline and key decisions |
| Body and notation | Geist for prose; Geist Mono for scope/tool notation | Geist task copy; mono only for hashes, tool names and measurements |
| Hero mechanism | Agent and execution areas divided by signed scope | Canvas demonstrates the protocol line, with live decision state |
| Controls | Squared orange CTA, simple directional arrows | Small-radius action buttons, consistent Phosphor icons |
| Content rhythm | Large statements, numbered participation steps, concise supporting copy | Short product statements; chronological event sequence with actual measurements |
| Tone | Verbs, concrete actions and explicit control decisions | Read, analyze, generate, approve; show deny and hold reasons |

Familiarity should come from the grammar, not borrowed identity. VeerIQ uses its own name and research symbol. It does not reuse ArmorIQ’s logo or imply an official product. The sponsor’s oversized marketing headline is adapted for a working app so the objective and evidence remain accessible. Light mode preserves the same grammar for research use; the judging demo defaults to the familiar dark view.

## What judges should remember

An analyst can finish the study even when an external document tries to widen its authority. Three separate facts appear together: the attempted export, the denial before dispatch, and the independent zero-byte measurement. A valid aggregate then pauses for a concrete approval and executes once. This separates task usefulness, containment, recovery and controlled action in a single product.

## Submission discipline

Spend remaining effort on live ArmorIQ proof, repeatable video, and clear documentation. Do not add a fleet dashboard, vector database, multi-cloud deployment, or unrelated tools. A live action that is stopped with independent evidence is more valuable than extra interface decoration.

Lead with the research outcome, show one actual boundary failure, then explain why permission to use a tool is broader than authorization for this task. End with the report and approved aggregate. Label deterministic replay, local signatures, application policy and sponsor policy explicitly.
